“As agents get more capable, why should your product still exist?”

[Jaclyn Konzelmann](<https://www.linkedin.com/in/jaclynkonzelmann/>) asked that at [The AI Conference](<https://www.linkedin.com/company/theaiconference/>) last week\. It's a question anyone building software product needs to be grappling with right now\.

Her example was Blender \- a tool she'd never used and hadn't had any intention to learn \- I made a similar example recently of Hubspot for myself\.

Because of the capability of her agents, she was able to create an animation that she wouldn't have otherwise\. Blender gained a user as a result\.

Blender is usually a pretty expert tool\. Honestly, one I've tinked with and shied away from as something I didn't have the patience to learn\. But the complexity of operation was completely mitigated by the LLM she used, and now that tool can reach a much wider audience\.

On the other hand, it puts some pressure on us as builders\. If a customer's agent can do the task, choose another tool, or just build a solution suitable to the task \- what do they need our product for?

The floor for a product utility is going to continue to rise\. Best to make sure you're continuing to ask that question as a part of your development journey\.
